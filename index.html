TL;DR: A hybrid quantum–classical framework for unsupervised PDE solving via spectral operator learning.

Abstract

Partial differential equations (PDEs) are central to modeling physical and engineering systems, but repeatedly solving parametric PDEs remains computationally expensive. Operator learning enables fast surrogate inference, yet typically requires large input–output paired datasets generated by costly high-fidelity PDE solvers. Unsupervised operator learning frameworks alleviate data dependency but remain hindered by computational bottlenecks. To address this limitation, we propose the Neural Variational Quantum Linear Solver (NVQLS), the first hybrid quantum–classical operator learning framework leveraging the Legendre–Galerkin weak formulation. We first resolve the sign ambiguity in VQLS energy minimization, preventing erroneous solution representations. Furthermore, we introduce a neural embedding, a novel encoding scheme that maps varying forcings and PDE coefficients into parameterized quantum circuit representations. These structural innovations improve per-iteration computational cost and memory scaling under suitable assumptions, while achieving superior accuracy compared to classical baselines. Validations on 1D, 2D and 3D parametric PDEs under diverse boundary conditions demonstrate NVQLS’s capability to simultaneously process varying inputs, offering a scalable unsupervised approach to quantum-enhanced operator learning.

Method

Phase-aware overlap cost

With a Legendre–Galerkin weak formulation, a PDE reduces to a linear system $A\alpha = F$ for the spectral coefficients $\alpha$ of its solution. The standard VQLS objective determines the solution state only up to a global phase, which can produce sign-flipped (reflected) PDE solutions. NVQLS instead uses the real part of the quantum state overlap:

$$\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{NVQLS}}(w)=\frac{1}{D}\sum_{i=1}^{D}\left(1-\frac{\mathrm{Re}\left(\sum_{l=1}^{L} c_{l}\,\langle F^{(i)}|A_{l}|\hat{\alpha}(f^{(i)};w)\rangle\right)}{\sqrt{\sum_{l=1}^{R} d_{l}\,\langle\hat{\alpha}(f^{(i)};w)|A_{l}|\hat{\alpha}(f^{(i)};w)\rangle}}\right),$$

where $A=\sum_{l} c_l A_l$ and $A^\dagger A=\sum_{l} d_l A_l$ are Pauli decompositions. The Pauli operators appear as observables measured at the end of the circuit, so commuting Pauli strings can be grouped and measured together.

Neural embedding for multiple instances

VQLS must be re-optimized for every new PDE instance. NVQLS introduces a classical angle network $g$ that maps each PDE input (forcing functions and coefficients) to circuit parameters, $|\hat{\alpha}\rangle = V\!\left(g(f^{(i)};w)\right)|0\rangle$, so a single model learns the solution operator across instances. The network output has dimension $\mathcal{O}(n^2)=\mathcal{O}((\log N^d)^2)$ for $n$ qubits.

The estimates rely on three scaling assumptions: an ansatz whose depth grows linearly with $n$ and that uses $\mathcal{O}(n^2)$ gate parameters is sufficiently expressive; forcing vectors can be prepared with a shallow embedding circuit of depth $\mathcal{O}(\log_2 N^d)$; and $\mathcal{O}(N^d\log_2 N^d)$ grouped measurement settings suffice per loss evaluation. These are supported by observations for the PDEs studied here, but are not guaranteed for general PDEs. The estimates are a methodological result and do not constitute evidence of quantum advantage.

Beyond 1D

  • 2D PDEs: mean relative $L_2$ errors of $3.8\times10^{-3}$ (RD), $2.1\times10^{-2}$ (Helmholtz), and $6.6\times10^{-3}$ (CD); $3.0\times10^{-3}$ with jointly varying coefficient–forcing inputs.
  • 3D elliptic PDEs: mean relative $L_2$ errors of $1.0$–$1.7\times10^{-2}$.
  • Discontinuous variable coefficients: $5.0\times10^{-4}$ for Darcy flow and $8.1\times10^{-3}$ for parabolic diffusion.

Limitations

Our experiments are small-scale noiseless classical simulations, so they validate the framework and its improved scaling under the stated assumptions rather than demonstrate a practical quantum advantage. In hardware-inspired noisy training on the 1D Helmholtz equation, the test relative $L_2$ error rises from $2.44\times10^{-3}$ (noiseless) to $2.68\times10^{-1}$ at device-informed error rates, and to $2.79\times10^{-2}$ at substantially reduced rates. Reducing circuit depth and error accumulation is an important direction for running NVQLS on quantum hardware.
